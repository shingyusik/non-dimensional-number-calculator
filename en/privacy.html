Privacy Policy

What information is processed when you use the site, and the choices you have.

Effective date: 22 October 2026

calctool.cc (Dimensionless Number Calculator, “the site”) is a personal project run by one individual. This policy explains what information is processed when you use the site, by whom and for what purpose, and what choices you have.

1. What the site does not do, and your calculator inputs

  • The site has no registration, sign-in or contact form, and does not ask for your name or email address.
  • Numbers you enter in a calculator are computed by JavaScript in your browser. The operator runs no server that receives or stores them.
  • However, the analytics, advertising and hosting/security services listed in section 2 are installed on the site and collect information from your browser automatically. In particular, on pages where Microsoft Clarity is installed, page layout and actions such as clicks and typing may be recorded for session replay.
  • Your theme (light or dark) and language choice are stored in your browser’s localStorage as theme and lang. They are not sent to the operator and are removed when you clear the site’s data in your browser.

3. Advertising and cookies

  • Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this website or other websites.
  • Google’s use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visits to this site and/or other sites on the Internet.
  • You may opt out of personalised advertising by visiting Google Ads Settings. Opting out does not remove ads; non-personalised ads may still appear.
  • Ads from third-party vendors other than Google may appear. You can opt out of some vendors’ personalised-advertising cookies at aboutads.info; users in Europe can also use Your Online Choices.
  • How Google uses information from sites that use its services is described at How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

4. Microsoft Clarity

The site uses Microsoft Clarity on the pages listed in section 2 to understand how the site is used and to improve usability. Clarity uses cookies and similar technologies to collect behavioural data such as clicks and scrolling and page information, and provides heatmaps and session replay. Third parties such as Microsoft may collect visitor information in this process, and Microsoft may collect or receive personal information to provide Microsoft Advertising. See the Microsoft Privacy Statement and Clarity cookie documentation.

5. Cloudflare

The site is hosted on Cloudflare Pages, and every request passes through Cloudflare, which processes IP addresses and traffic data. When the site’s responses were checked on 22 October 2026, Cloudflare set no cookies (no Set-Cookie header). Security features such as bot checks may still use Cloudflare security cookies when triggered. See the Cloudflare Privacy Policy and Cloudflare cookie documentation.

6. Retention and deletion

  • The operator keeps no server or database of visitor information, so there is no visitor data held by the operator to delete.
  • Information collected by each service is kept and deleted according to that service’s retention settings and policies: Google Analytics data retention, Clarity data retention, Google Privacy Policy, Cloudflare Privacy Policy.
  • Authors can edit or delete their own GitHub Issues posts; on request, the operator hides or deletes posts that contain personal information.

7. Third parties and international transfers

The operator does not compile visitor information to sell or hand over to anyone. The services in section 2, however, collect information directly from your browser through code installed on the site, and each company processes it under its own terms and privacy policy. The Microsoft Clarity terms treat the operator and Microsoft as independent controllers of personal data.

These companies process information outside Korea. Information is sent from your browser to each company over the internet when you open a page.

8. Cookies and similar technologies, and how to refuse them

A cookie is a small text file a website stores in your browser. On this site, the services in section 2 use cookies and similar technologies. Clarity’s documentation lists first-party cookies such as _clck and _clsk and third-party cookies such as CLID and MUID; whether they are actually set depends on the browser and environment.

  • Browser settings: block or delete cookies, for example following Chrome’s cookie help. Blocking only third-party cookies may leave analytics based on first-party cookies running.
  • Personalised advertising: Google Ads Settings, aboutads.info, Your Online Choices.
  • Google Analytics: the Google Analytics Opt-out Browser Add-on. It applies only to Google Analytics, not to Clarity, AdSense or Cloudflare.

The site currently has no cookie-consent management screen, so use the browser settings and service settings above to make your choices.

9. Your rights

You (or a legal guardian for a child under 14) may request access to, correction, deletion or suspension of processing of personal information, and may withdraw consent. Because the operator holds no visitor data directly, rights over data collected by Google and Microsoft are most quickly exercised through their account settings and privacy tools. To make a request to the operator, open an issue on GitHub Issues stating only the type of request; the operator will explain the process in the issue and review it without delay. GitHub Issues is public, so do not include personal information in the issue.

10. Privacy officer and complaints

The site is run by one individual and has no separate privacy department. The site operator is responsible for privacy matters, and privacy questions and complaints are received through GitHub Issues.

11. Security measures

The site is served over HTTPS. The operator keeps no server or database of visitor information, so there is no operator-side store of visitor data that could leak.

12. Changes to this policy

This policy applies from 22 October 2026 and replaces the English policy published in January 2026. Changes will be posted on this page with a new effective date, and earlier versions are available in the repository history. The Korean version at /privacy prevails if the two differ.
