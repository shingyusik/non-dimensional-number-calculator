Method and interpretation

Weber Number

See how to enter values, calculate a result, and check the limits of its interpretation.

When is it useful?

Weber number compares fluid inertia with surface tension. It is useful for droplets, bubbles, sprays, and other flows where a fluid interface affects shape or breakup.

Formula and inputs

We = ρv²L / σ

Enter density ρ in kg/m³, representative speed v in m/s, characteristic length L in m, and interfacial tension σ in N/m. Because speed is squared, doubling v multiplies We by four if the other inputs stay fixed.

Open this calculator

Worked example

For ρ = 1000 kg/m³, v = 2 m/s, L = 0.01 m, and σ = 0.072 N/m, We = 1000 × 2² × 0.01 ÷ 0.072 ≈ 555.56.

Interpreting the result

For this example and length scale, inertia is large relative to surface tension. The ratio alone does not establish a universal droplet-breakup threshold.

Limits and cautions

Choose σ for the relevant pair of fluids and temperature. A droplet diameter and a nozzle diameter define different Weber numbers; state which one you used.
