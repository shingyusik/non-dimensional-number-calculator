Method and interpretation

Prandtl Number

See how to enter values, calculate a result, and check the limits of its interpretation.

When is it useful?

Prandtl number compares momentum diffusivity with thermal diffusivity. It helps describe the relative behavior of velocity and thermal boundary layers in a fluid.

Formula and inputs

Pr = μcₚ / k

Enter dynamic viscosity μ in Pa·s, constant-pressure specific heat cₚ in J/(kg·K), and thermal conductivity k in W/(m·K), all evaluated at a consistent state. The equivalent form Pr = ν/α uses kinematic viscosity ν and thermal diffusivity α.

Worked example

For illustrative properties μ = 0.001 Pa·s, cₚ = 4180 J/(kg·K), and k = 0.6 W/(m·K), Pr = 0.001 × 4180 ÷ 0.6 ≈ 6.97. These sample values do not replace design-property tables.

Interpreting the result

A value above 1 means momentum diffusivity exceeds thermal diffusivity under this definition. Pr by itself does not determine the convective coefficient or heat-transfer rate.

Limits and cautions

All three properties can depend on temperature. Do not mix values taken at different states or substitute kinematic viscosity ν for dynamic viscosity μ.
