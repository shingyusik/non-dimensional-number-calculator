Method and interpretation

Nusselt Number

See how to enter values, calculate a result, and check the limits of its interpretation.

When is it useful?

Nusselt number compares surface convective heat transfer with conduction through the fluid over a chosen length scale. It helps compare heat-transfer conditions when the geometry and definitions are consistent.

Formula and inputs

Nu = hL / k

Enter convective heat-transfer coefficient h in W/(m²·K), characteristic length L in m, and fluid thermal conductivity k in W/(m·K). The coefficient h must come from a measurement or a separate correlation; this calculator does not predict it.

Open this calculator

Worked example

With h = 50 W/(m²·K), L = 0.1 m, and k = 0.6 W/(m·K), Nu = 50 × 0.1 ÷ 0.6 ≈ 8.33. The units cancel when entered consistently.

Interpreting the result

For the same geometry, length definition, and fluid-property basis, a larger Nu indicates a larger convective coefficient relative to conduction. Nu is neither heat flux nor total heat transferred.

Limits and cautions

Choose L for the geometry, such as pipe diameter or distance along a plate. An average Nu based on average h is not directly interchangeable with a local Nu.
