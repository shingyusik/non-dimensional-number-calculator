Method and interpretation

Reynolds Number

See how to enter values, calculate a result, and check the limits of its interpretation.

When is it useful?

Reynolds number compares the relative importance of inertia and viscosity. It helps compare flow inside a pipe or around an object, but the same value can mean something different if the geometry or reference length changes.

Formula and inputs

Re = ρvL / μ

Enter density ρ in kg/m³, flow speed v in m/s, characteristic length L in m, and dynamic viscosity μ in Pa·s. For flow inside a circular pipe, L is usually its inner diameter. If you have kinematic viscosity ν in m²/s instead, use Re = vL/ν; do not enter ν as μ.

Worked example

For an illustrative circular pipe, let ρ = 1000 kg/m³, v = 0.2 m/s, L = 0.01 m, and μ = 0.001 Pa·s. Then Re = 1000 × 0.2 × 0.01 ÷ 0.001 = 2000. The units cancel.

Interpreting the result

With mean velocity and inner diameter for this pipe example, 2000 is approximately in the laminar-flow range. The calculator’s 2300 and 4000 boundaries are simplified guidance for internal pipe flow.

Limits and cautions

Do not carry the pipe thresholds over to a pipe entrance, bend, rough wall, or another geometry unchanged. Use viscosity measured or tabulated at the relevant temperature.
