Reading St as a ratio of times

St = fL/v is the transit time L/v divided by the oscillation period 1/f. St = 0.2 means that while the fluid travels one cylinder diameter, the oscillation advances one fifth of a cycle. Expressed this way, frequencies from experiments of different size and speed collapse to a single comparable number.

Rearranged as f = St·v/L, it gives a rough frequency estimate when St is known. At constant St, doubling the speed doubles the frequency.

Entering the frequency

  • f is in hertz (cycles per second). Divide rpm by 60 to get Hz.
  • An angular frequency ω in rad/s must be divided by 2π. Entering ω directly makes St about 6.28 times too large.
  • Behind a cylinder, lift fluctuates at the shedding frequency, while drag commonly fluctuates at twice that frequency. Record which signal f was read from.
  • Use the diameter for a cylinder, or the width across the flow for other bodies.

How to treat the value 0.2

Vortex shedding behind a circular cylinder is known to give St around 0.2 over a fairly wide range of Reynolds numbers. It still varies with Re, surface condition and free-stream disturbances, so do not treat it as a universal constant. When comparing with published values, match the geometry, the definition of L, the measured signal and the Re of the experiment.

The example and the interface note

The example uses f = 10 Hz, a cylinder diameter L = 0.1 m and v = 5 m/s, giving St = 0.2. The surface-tension note reaches the same 0.2 with a 2 mm length, 0.5 m/s and 50 Hz, as the ratio of a 0.004 s transit time to a 0.02 s period. A zero speed or frequency leaves nothing to compare or divides by zero, so the calculator reports such inputs as errors.
