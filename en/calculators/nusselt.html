What Nu is a ratio of

Nu = hL/k can be read as h divided by k/L. The quantity k/L is the heat-transfer coefficient of a stagnant fluid layer of thickness L conducting heat with no flow, so Nu says how many times stronger the actual convection is than conduction over the same length.

Nu near 1 means convection is comparable to conduction; larger values mean the flow is doing much of the heat transport. The number changes with the choice of L, so comparisons only make sense between cases that define L the same way.

Where does h come from?

h comes from measurements or from a validated correlation for the geometry and flow. Correlations usually give Nu as a function of the Reynolds and Prandtl numbers, so in practice Nu is found first and converted back with h = Nu·k/L. This page works in the other direction: when you already have h, it gives Nu for checking against a reference or another case.

Units and length definitions

  • h is in W/(m²·K) and k in W/(m·K). The difference between m² and m cancels with L, leaving Nu dimensionless.
  • Older data in kcal/(h·m²·°C) convert with 1 kcal/h = 1.163 W: multiply by 1.163 for W/(m²·K). A temperature difference in °C equals the same difference in K.
  • k is the conductivity of the fluid, not of the solid wall. The Biot number has the same form but uses the wall’s conductivity, which makes the two easy to confuse.
  • Use the inner diameter for pipes; for plates use the distance from the leading edge (local Nu) or the plate length (mean Nu).

Comparing results

The example uses h = 50 W/(m²·K), L = 0.1 m and k = 0.6 W/(m·K), giving Nu ≈ 8.33. Keep h and k but change L to 0.05 m and Nu halves to about 4.17. Heat transfer did not weaken; only the reference length changed, so never compare Nu values built on different L. A mean Nu from an average h is also a different quantity from a local Nu at one position.

The thermal and mass diffusion note works through h = 120 W/(m²·K), L = 0.05 m and k = 0.6 W/(m·K) for Nu = 10, and shows that a heat flux additionally needs a temperature difference.
