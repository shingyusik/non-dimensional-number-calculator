Comparing advection and diffusion as times

Pe = vL/α is easiest to read as a ratio of times. The flow crosses a length L in the transit time L/v, while diffusion needs a time of order L²/α to spread over the same length. Dividing the diffusion time by the transit time gives vL/α. When Pe is much larger than 1, heat is carried away by the flow before it can spread; when it is much smaller, diffusion wins.

In the pipe-flow note the transit time is 0.05 s and the diffusion time 700 s, a ratio of 14000. Both times are scales built on the chosen L, not predictions of how long heating takes.

When α is not given directly

  • Compute α = k/(ρcₚ). The water-like example in the pipe-flow note gives 0.6 ÷ (1000 × 4200) ≈ 1.43×10⁻⁷ m²/s.
  • Data in mm²/s convert to m²/s by multiplying by 10⁻⁶.
  • With the same L, v and properties, Re × Pr gives the same number, which makes a handy cross-check.
  • Pick v and L to match the direction you care about: the diameter for diffusion across a pipe, or the pipe length for transport along it.

Using it as a mass Péclet number

Enter the molecular diffusivity D of a species (m²/s) in the α field and the calculator returns vL/D, the mass Péclet number, which equals Re × Sc. Read “thermal diffusion” in the explanation as “mass diffusion”. Because D in liquids is usually much smaller than α, the mass Péclet number is often far larger than the thermal one in the same flow.

Interpreting the size

The example uses v = 0.01 m/s, L = 0.1 m and α = 1×10⁻⁷ m²/s, giving Pe = 10,000. Around Pe ≈ 1 both advection and diffusion matter, but no single number is a universal boundary. In turbulent flow, mixing by eddies can be far stronger than molecular diffusion, so a Pe built on molecular α should not be read as the actual temperature field or heat-transfer rate. Surface heat transfer is handled separately through the Nusselt number and h.
